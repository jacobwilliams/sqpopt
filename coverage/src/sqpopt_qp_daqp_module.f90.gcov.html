       5              : !  The QP subproblem solved by DAQP (`options%qp_solver_mode = sqpopt_qp_daqp`):
       6              : !  the dual active-set solver for dense convex QPs of D. Arnström, A.
       7              : !  Bemporad, and D. Axehill (*A dual active-set solver for embedded quadratic
       8              : !  programming using recursive LDLᵀ updates*, IEEE Transactions on Automatic
       9              : !  Control 67(8), 2022), through its Fortran port, the `daqp-fortran` fpm
      10              : !  package (`daqp_module`).
      11              : !
      12              : !  Like the dense QP solver ([[sqpopt_qp_dense_module]]), it forms the dense
      13              : !  Hessian and Jacobian, and solves
      14              : !
      15              : !  $$ \min_p \; \tfrac12 p^T H p + g^T p \quad \text{s.t.} \quad
      16              : !     x_l - x \le p \le x_u - x, \quad c_l - c \le J p \le c_u - c $$
      17              : !
      18              : !  with the variable bounds as DAQP's simple bounds (which it handles without
      19              : !  forming their rows), and the linearized constraints as its general rows.
      20              : !  DAQP factors \( H \) once per QP (Cholesky), and then updates the
      21              : !  \( LDL^T \) factors of the working set's Gram matrix as rows enter and leave
      22              : !  it, where the dense solver refactors at every iteration. Being a dual
      23              : !  method, it needs no feasible starting point.
      24              : !
      25              : !  It only solves *convex* QPs with *consistent* constraints. When DAQP
      26              : !  doesn't solve the QP (an indefinite \( H \), inconsistent linearized
      27              : !  constraints, cycling, the iteration limit, ...), [[solve_daqp_qp]] says so,
      28              : !  and [[sqpopt_qp_solver_module]] solves it with the dense active-set solver
      29              : !  instead, which has the elastic mode and follows negative curvature; the
      30              : !  forced elastic re-solves go to the dense solver directly. A singular
      31              : !  \( H \) goes to the dense solver too: DAQP's proximal-point loop for
      32              : !  semidefinite Hessians is turned off (`eps_prox = 0`), because on the
      33              : !  Hock-Schittkowski problems it treated indefinite SR1 Hessians as
      34              : !  semidefinite and ran into the iteration limit (17 times the time, and
      35              : !  worse results), and did worse with the exact Hessian too.
      36              : !
      37              : !  **Warm start.** Each QP starts from the working set given (the previous
      38              : !  QP's final one, which this solver shares with the dense one, see
      39              : !  [[working_set]]), mapped to DAQP's numbering; DAQP drops the rows of it
      40              : !  that are linearly dependent on the others. Otherwise (after `setup`), it
      41              : !  starts from the equality constraints and fixed variables.
      42              : !
