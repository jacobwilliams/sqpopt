       5              : !  Dense matrix-vector and matrix-matrix products, in the caller's arrays:
       6              : !  the library's replacement for the `matmul` intrinsic, which it doesn't
       7              : !  use. An expression with `matmul` (nested, or with a `transpose`, or as an
       8              : !  actual argument) needs an array temporary for its result, and some
       9              : !  compilers put those on the stack (the Intel compilers by default,
      10              : !  gfortran with `-fstack-arrays` or `-Ofast`), which a moderately large
      11              : !  matrix then overflows (Windows's default stack is 1 MB, the size of a
      12              : !  350 by 350 matrix). These routines form nothing: the result goes into
      13              : !  an array the caller has allocated, and `op(A)` is never formed.
      14              : !
      15              : !  The products are explicit loops (column-oriented, the order of the
      16              : !  arrays' storage), in any real kind; in a library built with LAPACK (the
      17              : !  `HAS_LAPACK` preprocessor directive, in double precision, which links
      18              : !  BLAS too), BLAS's `DGEMV` and `DGEMM` instead, whenever the arrays are
      19              : !  contiguous (a section that isn't, e.g. the leading rows of a matrix,
      20              : !  uses the loops, since BLAS would need it copied). The two builds'
      21              : !  products then differ in rounding, and so can their iterates.
