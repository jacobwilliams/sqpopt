       5              : !  Nonlinear least-squares problems, solved with the SQP solver
       6              : !  ([[sqpopt_nlls_type]]): an optional layer on top of [[sqpopt_type]],
       7              : !  which the rest of the library doesn't know about.
       8              : !
       9              : !  The problem is
      10              : !
      11              : !  $$ \min_x \; \tfrac12 \sum_{i=1}^{l} r_i(x)^2 \quad \text{s.t.} \quad
      12              : !     c_l \le c(x) \le c_u, \quad x_l \le x \le x_u $$
      13              : !
      14              : !  for residuals \( r_i \) (e.g. the differences between a model and
      15              : !  data) and, optionally, constraints \( c \). It is solved in the form
      16              : !  proposed by Schittkowski (the code DFNLP): with a new variable
      17              : !  \( z_i \) for each residual,
      18              : !
      19              : !  $$ \min_{x,z} \; \tfrac12 z^T z \quad \text{s.t.} \quad r(x) - z = 0,
      20              : !     \quad c_l \le c(x) \le c_u, \quad x_l \le x \le x_u $$
      21              : !
      22              : !  which is an ordinary nonlinear program for [[sqpopt_type]], in
      23              : !  \( n + l \) variables and \( l + m \) constraints. Its QP subproblems
      24              : !  are always consistent as far as the residuals go (any step in \( x \)
      25              : !  has a \( z \) to go with it), and their step is a Gauss-Newton step
      26              : !  with a correction from the Hessian approximation, as in the special
      27              : !  codes for least-squares problems.
      28              : !
      29              : !  **Use it** for data fitting, and for a system of equations that can't
      30              : !  all hold (more equations than unknowns): given as equality constraints
      31              : !  to [[sqpopt_type]] they make every QP subproblem inconsistent, and the
      32              : !  solve ends as `sqpopt_infeasible` at best, after many restoration
      33              : !  steps.
      34              : !
      35              : !  **The Hessian.** With the quasi-Newton modes of `options%hessian_mode`
      36              : !  (the default), the solver approximates the Hessian of the transformed
      37              : !  problem as it does any other. With `sqpopt_hessian_exact`, this module
      38              : !  supplies the *Gauss-Newton* Hessian: the identity on \( z \), and zero
      39              : !  on \( x \) (the second derivatives of the residuals and of the
      40              : !  constraints are neglected, as in the Gauss-Newton method). The solver's
      41              : !  Hessian shift then acts as the damping of the Levenberg-Marquardt
      42              : !  method. It usually takes fewer iterations, mostly so when the residuals
      43              : !  are small at the solution, but it fails more often.
      44              : !
      45              : !  **What to expect.** On 94 overdetermined systems of equations from
      46              : !  CUTEst (at most 250 iterations), the default L-BFGS reached the best
      47              : !  sum of squares found on 59, with 6,842 residual evaluations in all, and
      48              : !  the Gauss-Newton Hessian on 68, with 13,154 (one or the other: 79).
      49              : !  Given to [[sqpopt_type]] as equality constraints: 52, with 52,958. A
      50              : !  code written for least squares does better on such problems (scipy's
      51              : !  `least_squares`: 87, with 10,326), so prefer one (e.g. MINPACK) for an
      52              : !  unconstrained fit that is hard. What this interface adds is the
      53              : !  solver's constraints and sparsity. It is a layer on the solver, not a
      54              : !  least-squares method of its own: the solver is unchanged by it.
