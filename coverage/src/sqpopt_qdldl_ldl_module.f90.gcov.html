       6              : !  (`options%linear_solver = sqpopt_linear_solver_qdldl`, the default):
       7              : !  [QDLDL](https://github.com/jacobwilliams/qdldl-fortran), a Fortran port
       8              : !  of the \( LDL^T \) solver of the OSQP QP solver, an fpm dependency, so it
       9              : !  is always available, in any real kind. It factors in a fill-reducing
      10              : !  order (approximate minimum degree, with dense rows last), *without
      11              : !  pivoting*, on one thread, with very little overhead per call.
      12              : !
      13              : !  Without pivoting, it is exact for quasi-definite matrices, and for the
      14              : !  KKT matrices \( \begin{bmatrix} H & J^T \\ J & 0 \end{bmatrix} \) with
      15              : !  \( H \) positive definite once each constraint's row comes after its
      16              : !  variables (the `signs` of [[qdldl_start]], see [[delay_negative_rows]]):
      17              : !  the least-squares systems, and the matrices of the quasi-Newton
      18              : !  Hessians. For an indefinite \( H \) the inertia is still exact as long
      19              : !  as no pivot is zero (Sylvester's law).
      20              : !
      21              : !  **Null pivots.** A pivot of at most `qdldl_zero_tol` times the largest
      22              : !  element is null. The first one stops a first factorization, which is
      23              : !  then repeated with each null pivot replaced by a large one (which makes
      24              : !  its row and column of the factors negligible, so that a solve returns
      25              : !  one of the solutions of a consistent system, as with MUMPS). Whether
      26              : !  those factors are still the matrix's is then checked (see
      27              : !  [[qdldl_faithful]]): they are if the matrix is singular, and `n_null`
      28              : !  counts its zero eigenvalues. If they aren't, the zero pivot was an
      29              : !  artifact of the order (e.g. a Hessian with zeros on its diagonal, which
      30              : !  would need a 2x2 pivot), and the inertia is unknown:
      31              : !  `inertia_known = .false.` (what that means for the optimization is
      32              : !  decided by [[sqpopt_kkt_module]]).
