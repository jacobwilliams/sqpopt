       5              : !  The dense backend of [[sqpopt_symmetric_solver_module]]
       6              : !  (`options%linear_solver = sqpopt_linear_solver_dense`), for small
       7              : !  matrices: the matrix is stored dense (order `n`, so \( n^2 \) elements),
       8              : !  its inertia is the exact count of [[dense_symmetric_inertia]]
       9              : !  (Householder tridiagonalization, then the signs of a Sturm sequence, with
      10              : !  2 by 2 pivots for coupled zeros, as Bunch-Kaufman's), and solves use an
      11              : !  LU factorization with partial pivoting. So, unlike QDLDL, it always
      12              : !  knows the inertia, also of a Hessian with zeros on its diagonal, and,
      13              : !  unlike MUMPS, it has almost no overhead per call. But each factorization
      14              : !  costs \( O(n^3) \): it is meant for matrices of order up to a few hundred,
      15              : !  and refuses to start above `dense_max_order` (the factorization options
      16              : !  then fall back on the matrix-free methods).
      17              : !
      18              : !  A singular matrix is not an error: its zero eigenvalues are counted
      19              : !  (`n_null`), and in the LU factorization a null column gets a large
      20              : !  pivot, which makes that component of a solution zero, so that a solve
      21              : !  returns one of the solutions of a consistent system (as MUMPS's
      22              : !  null-pivot detection does).
