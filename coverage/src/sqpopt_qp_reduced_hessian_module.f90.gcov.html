       5              : !  **Sparse** active-set QP solver for the linearized SQP subproblem (the
       6              : !  default for larger problems, see `sqpopt_qp_auto`). Like
       7              : !  [[sqpopt_qp_dense_module]], this enforces the linearized constraints
       8              : !  and bounds exactly, but never forms a dense matrix: the Hessian is only
       9              : !  used through products, and active variable bounds simply fix their
      10              : !  variables. The null space of the working set is handled in one of two
      11              : !  ways (`null_space`):
      12              : !
      13              : !  * `sqpopt_null_space_lu` (the default, SQOPT-style): every general row
      14              : !    gets a slack variable (\( J p - s = 0 \)), so all the constraints
      15              : !    are simple bounds on the unknowns, and the working set is the set of
      16              : !    unknowns fixed at a bound. The free unknowns are split into a square
      17              : !    nonsingular **basis** `B` (sparse LU factors, from `LUSOL`) and the
      18              : !    **superbasic** rest `S`, so the null space is spanned by
      19              : !    \( Z = [-B^{-1} S; I] \). Every product with `Z` or \( Z^T \) is
      20              : !    one solve with `B`'s factors, the reduced-space Newton system
      21              : !    \( Z^T H Z \, d_S = -Z^T (Hv+g) \) is solved by conjugate gradients,
      22              : !    and the multipliers come from one solve with \( B^T \). All of these
      23              : !    are direct (not iterative) solves. Fixing or freeing a superbasic
      24              : !    unknown leaves `B` unchanged; fixing a basic one swaps in a superbasic
      25              : !    column with a Bartels-Golub update of the factors (`lu8rpc`), so `B` is
      26              : !    only refactorized occasionally. Also:
      27              : !
      28              : !    - the starting step is a *basic* solution of the working-set guess
      29              : !      (one LU solve), not a minimum-norm one;
      30              : !    - CG is preconditioned by the diagonal of the (L-BFGS) Hessian, and a
      31              : !      face with few superbasics (`dense_max_ns`) is solved exactly with a
      32              : !      dense Cholesky factorization of the reduced Hessian instead;
      33              : !    - when a CG step is blocked by a superbasic, a gradient-projection
      34              : !      step fixes every superbasic it takes past a bound at once (if the
      35              : !      basics stay feasible and the objective decreases, with
      36              : !      backtracking), and at a face optimum every wrongly-signed fixed
      37              : !      unknown is freed at once (the worst one only, in the second half of
      38              : !      the iteration limit, against cycling). Both matter on infeasible
      39              : !      starts, where many constraints change status in one QP.
      40              : !  * `sqpopt_null_space_lsqr`: orthogonal projections onto the null space,
      41              : !    each a least-squares solve with `LSQR`, with **projected conjugate
      42              : !    gradients** (Gould, Hribar & Nocedal 1998; Nocedal & Wright,
      43              : !    *Numerical Optimization*, Ch. 16).
      44              : !
      45              : !  The formulation is the same as the dense solver's: general constraints
      46              : !  and bounds are uniform two-sided "rows" (stored in compressed-row form,
      47              : !  so each row's product costs only its own nonzeros), and every general
      48              : !  constraint violated at `p=0` gets an elastic slack with an \( \ell_1 \)
      49              : !  penalty, so the starting step plus those slacks is feasible, and
      50              : !  inconsistent linearized constraints are detected (the penalty weight is
      51              : !  raised up to `elastic_weight_max`, then `istat=sqpopt_infeasible`); see
      52              : !  [[sqpopt_qp_dense_module]] for the details, and for the forced elastic
      53              : !  mode, which works the same way here (here, the slacks also get a
      54              : !  small proximal curvature, so CG steps along them stay bounded). Projected
      55              : !  CG stops on
      56              : !  (relative) convergence, and follows any direction of nonpositive
      57              : !  curvature (e.g. along an elastic slack, or an indefinite SR1 Hessian) to
      58              : !  the nearest blocking row. The initial working set is a linearly
      59              : !  independent subset of the rows at a bound, picked by one rank-revealing
      60              : !  sparse LU factorization (`LUSOL`); every row added after that is
      61              : !  independent by construction (a blocking row is never in the span of
      62              : !  the working set).
