       5              : !  A direct solution of the QP subproblem (`options%direct_qp`), by sparse
       6              : !  factorizations of the KKT matrix of its working set (see
       7              : !  [[sqpopt_kkt_module]]; MUMPS or QDLDL, see `options%linear_solver`).
       8              : !
       9              : !  For a given working set (the general rows and variable bounds held at
      10              : !  a bound), the QP is an equality-constrained one, and its solution is one
      11              : !  linear solve:
      12              : !
      13              : !  $$ \begin{bmatrix} H & J_a^T \\ J_a & 0 \end{bmatrix}
      14              : !     \begin{bmatrix} p \\ -\lambda \end{bmatrix} =
      15              : !     \begin{bmatrix} -g \\ b_a - c_a \end{bmatrix} $$
      16              : !
      17              : !  with the variables at a bound of the working set fixed there. That step
      18              : !  solves the whole QP if it is *feasible* (it satisfies the rows and bounds
      19              : !  outside the working set) and *optimal* (the multipliers of the working
      20              : !  set's inequalities have the right signs), and the face is convex (no
      21              : !  negative curvature, which the factorization tells).
      22              : !
      23              : !  [[direct_qp_step]] starts from the working set that the QP solver would
      24              : !  start from: near a solution of the nonlinear problem, that is already
      25              : !  the right one, and the QP costs one factorization and one solve. If the
      26              : !  step isn't feasible and optimal, the working set is changed as in a
      27              : !  *primal-dual active-set method* (Hintermüller, Ito & Kunisch, 2002):
      28              : !  every row and bound the step violates is added, every one whose
      29              : !  multiplier has the wrong sign is dropped, and the new face is factored
      30              : !  and solved. After a few changes without success, it gives up, and the
      31              : !  caller solves the QP with its active-set solver instead (see
      32              : !  [[solve_qp_subproblem]]). So the direct method only ever replaces a QP
      33              : !  solve by a solution that satisfies the QP's optimality conditions.
      34              : !
      35              : !  Adding every violated bound at once can leave a row of the working set
      36              : !  with no free variable, or otherwise dependent on the others: the KKT
      37              : !  matrix is then singular. Such a face is factored again with a small
      38              : !  regularization (\( -\epsilon I \) in the rows' block, see
      39              : !  [[sqpopt_kkt_module]]), which satisfies the dependent rows only in a
      40              : !  heavily weighted least-squares sense. That gives them large multipliers,
      41              : !  so the bounds that over-determine them are dropped at the next change.
      42              : !  A step is only accepted if it satisfies the working set's rows, so a
      43              : !  regularized face is accepted only if its rows were consistent. A
      44              : !  regularized solve leaves each row short by \( \epsilon \) times its
      45              : !  multiplier, so if that is all that is wrong with the step, the face is
      46              : !  solved once more with a much smaller \( \epsilon \). (That matters
      47              : !  when the solver finds a matrix singular that isn't: near the solution of
      48              : !  a chain of 100,000 circle constraints, the first regularization left the
      49              : !  rows `7e-8` short, and the method gave up on a QP it had solved.) A face
      50              : !  that is still singular (a direction of zero curvature) ends the method.
      51              : !
      52              : !  A face with negative curvature has no minimizer. With inertia control
      53              : !  (see [[sqpopt_inertia_module]]), the Hessian's shift is raised until the
      54              : !  face is convex, and the method continues from there with the shifted
      55              : !  Hessian (the shift only ever increases, so this can't cycle). Without
      56              : !  it, the method gives up at such a face.
      57              : !
      58              : !  The factorization can't be updated when the working set changes: every
      59              : !  change is a new factorization. That is cheap next to the active-set
      60              : !  solvers on large problems (their iterations each solve with the reduced
      61              : !  Hessian), but it is why this isn't used for a QP that needs many
      62              : !  changes, such as the first one from an infeasible starting point.
