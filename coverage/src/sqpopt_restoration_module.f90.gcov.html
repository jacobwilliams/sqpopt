       5              : !  Feasibility restoration, used by [[sqpopt_iterate_module]] when the QP
       6              : !  subproblem reports that the linearized constraints are inconsistent
       7              : !  (`sqpopt_infeasible`), so that no QP step can be trusted, or when the
       8              : !  filter or funnel line search, or the trust region, finds no acceptable
       9              : !  step at an infeasible point (one whose largest constraint violation, as
      10              : !  the convergence test measures it, exceeds `options%ctol`: a round-off
      11              : !  violation has nothing to restore). Two strategies are available
      12              : !  (`options%restoration_mode`):
      13              : !
      14              : !  * `sqpopt_restoration_phase` (**default**): when the filter or funnel
      15              : !    line search, or the trust region, finds no acceptable step, a
      16              : !    **feasibility restoration phase**, as in filter-SQP methods (Fletcher
      17              : !    & Leyffer; Wächter & Biegler; and the Uno solver's
      18              : !    `FeasibilityRestoration`). (An inconsistent QP is still handled by the
      19              : !    Gauss-Newton step below, falling back to the QP's elastic step, which
      20              : !    also accounts for the objective: on the Hock-Schittkowski problems
      21              : !    this finds better local solutions than a pure feasibility phase.) The
      22              : !    solver switches to minimizing the constraint violation for as many
      23              : !    major iterations as needed (see [[restoration_phase_step]]): each one
      24              : !    solves a *feasibility QP* with the regular QP solvers, whose objective
      25              : !    is a proximal term toward the point where the phase started, and
      26              : !    whose linearized constraints are enforced (or, if they are
      27              : !    inconsistent, their \( \ell_1 \) violation is minimized by the QP's
      28              : !    elastic mode), followed by a backtracking search on the \( \ell_1 \)
      29              : !    violation. The phase ends (see [[restoration_phase_done]]) once the
      30              : !    violation has dropped below `restoration_exit_factor` times its value
      31              : !    at the start of the phase *and* the point is acceptable to the filter
      32              : !    (or funnel), when the point is feasible, after
      33              : !    `restoration_max_iter` iterations, or when a phase step (and its
      34              : !    Gauss-Newton fallback) fails, so that the next iteration tries the
      35              : !    optimality QP again. The point where the phase started
      36              : !    is added to the filter (or the funnel is tightened toward it), so the
      37              : !    iterations can't cycle back to it.
      38              : !  * `sqpopt_restoration_gauss_newton`: a single Gauss-Newton step on the
      39              : !    constraint violation each time (the original, lightweight strategy;
      40              : !    with the filter or funnel line search, the current point is added to
      41              : !    the filter, or the funnel tightened, first):
      42              : !
      43              : !  $$ \min_x \; \tfrac12 \lVert r_c(x) \rVert_2^2 \quad \text{s.t.} \quad x_l \le x \le x_u $$
      44              : !
      45              : !  where \( r_c \) is the signed violation of each constraint's bounds.
      46              : !  Repeated restoration steps converge to either a feasible point (after
      47              : !  which the normal SQP iterations resume) or a point that is stationary
      48              : !  for the violation, which [[check_convergence]] then reports as
      49              : !  `sqpopt_infeasible`. This step is also the fallback when a restoration
      50              : !  phase step fails.
      51              : !
      52              : !  A problem with more equality constraints than variables has an
      53              : !  inconsistent QP at every iteration, and is solved by these steps alone
      54              : !  (see [[sqpopt_nlls_module]] for a better way to pose one whose
      55              : !  equations can't all hold).
      56              : !
      57              : !  In both cases, before a point that is stationary for the violation is
      58              : !  reported as infeasible, [[escape_step]] looks for a second-order
      59              : !  decrease of the violation.
