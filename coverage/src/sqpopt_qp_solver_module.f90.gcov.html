       5              : !  Solves the quadratic programming (QP) subproblem generated at each
       6              : !  major SQP iteration:
       7              : !
       8              : !  $$ \min_{p} \; \frac{1}{2} p^T H p + g^T p $$
       9              : !
      10              : !  subject to the linearized constraints and variable bounds:
      11              : !
      12              : !  $$ c_l - c(x) \le J(x) p \le c_u - c(x) $$
      13              : !  $$ x_l - x \le p \le x_u - x $$
      14              : !
      15              : !  The subproblem is solved by one of two active-set QP solvers, selected
      16              : !  by `mode`:
      17              : !
      18              : !  * `sqpopt_qp_dense`: a dense active-set QP (see [[sqpopt_qp_dense_module]]);
      19              : !  * `sqpopt_qp_reduced_hessian`: a sparse/matrix-free active-set QP (see
      20              : !    [[sqpopt_qp_reduced_hessian_module]]);
      21              : !  * `sqpopt_qp_auto` (the default): `sqpopt_qp_dense` when
      22              : !    `n <= auto_dense_max_n`, else `sqpopt_qp_reduced_hessian`.
      23              : !
      24              : !  Before either is run, the *unconstrained step* is tried (unless
      25              : !  `unconstrained_step` is false): with the limited-memory BFGS Hessian,
      26              : !  which is positive definite, the minimizer of the QP's objective alone is
      27              : !  \( p = -H^{-1} g \), which the two-loop recursion gives in `O(nk)`
      28              : !  operations for `k` stored pairs (see [[hessian_inverse_vector_product]]).
      29              : !  If that step satisfies the bounds and the linearized constraints, it is
      30              : !  the solution of the QP, with zero multipliers, and no QP solver is run.
      31              : !  That is every QP of a problem without constraints whose bounds aren't
      32              : !  active, where the active-set solvers would otherwise work with every
      33              : !  variable free (the sparse one by conjugate gradients on a reduced
      34              : !  Hessian of order `n`). When the step isn't feasible, the attempt costs
      35              : !  one such product.
      36              : !
      37              : !  With `direct` (`options%direct_qp`), the
      38              : !  subproblem is then tried directly, by factoring the KKT matrix of the
      39              : !  working set that the active-set solver would start from (see
      40              : !  [[sqpopt_qp_direct_module]]); the active-set solver is only run if that
      41              : !  doesn't give the solution within `direct_max_changes` changes of the
      42              : !  working set.
      43              : !
      44              : !  Both enforce the variable bounds and the linearized constraints exactly
      45              : !  as part of the QP solve. The step length is then capped at
      46              : !  `max_step*step_scale` (a trust-region-style safeguard), where the major
      47              : !  iterations adapt `step_scale` like a trust radius: it doubles after a
      48              : !  capped step that the line search accepted in full, and halves (down to
      49              : !  1) after a shortened one. Each solve starts with
      50              : !  `step_scale = max(1, ||x0||_inf/max_step)`, so the cap starts at
      51              : !  \( \max(\) `max_step` \(, \lVert x_0 \rVert_\infty) \): relative to the
      52              : !  size of the variables, and a solution far away is still reached in a
      53              : !  few iterations.
