       5              : !  The KKT matrix of a QP subproblem's working set, factored by the sparse
       6              : !  direct solver of [[sqpopt_symmetric_solver_module]] (MUMPS or QDLDL,
       7              : !  see `options%linear_solver`):
       8              : !
       9              : !  $$ K = \begin{bmatrix} H & J_a^T \\ J_a & -\epsilon I \end{bmatrix} $$
      10              : !
      11              : !  where \( H \) is the Hessian in the *free* variables (those not at a
      12              : !  bound of the working set), \( J_a \) holds the working set's rows of the
      13              : !  Jacobian in those variables, and \( \epsilon \ge 0 \) is an optional
      14              : !  regularization (zero, unless a singular matrix is being regularized).
      15              : !
      16              : !  It answers two questions about the QP restricted to that working set
      17              : !  (a *face* of the QP):
      18              : !
      19              : !  * **Is it convex there?** By Sylvester's law of inertia, \( K \) has one
      20              : !    negative eigenvalue for each (independent) row of \( J_a \), plus one
      21              : !    for each negative eigenvalue of the reduced Hessian \( Z^THZ \). So
      22              : !    `n_negative`, the number of negative eigenvalues beyond the number of
      23              : !    rows, counts the directions of negative curvature on the face. This is
      24              : !    what the inertia control uses (see [[sqpopt_inertia_module]]).
      25              : !  * **Where is its minimizer?** One solve with the factors gives the step
      26              : !    to the minimizer of the face, and its multipliers (see
      27              : !    [[sqpopt_qp_direct_module]]).
      28              : !
      29              : !  **A fixed pattern.** So that the sparsity pattern is the same for every
      30              : !  working set (it is analysed only once per solve), \( K \) always has
      31              : !  order `n+m`: a variable at a bound of the working set has its row and
      32              : !  column replaced by those of the identity, and a constraint outside it by
      33              : !  those of minus the identity. The unknowns of a solve are then all `n`
      34              : !  step components followed by all `m` multipliers (see [[kkt_solve]]).
      35              : !
      36              : !  **One factorization at a time.** The last factorization is kept, with
      37              : !  the working set it was made for. Asking for the same one again (while
      38              : !  the matrices haven't changed, see [[kkt_new_matrices]]) costs nothing,
      39              : !  so the inertia control and the direct QP step share their
      40              : !  factorizations.
      41              : !
      42              : !  The Hessian is the solver's ([[kkt_factor]]), or a multiple of the
      43              : !  identity ([[kkt_factor_identity]], for the least-squares problems of
      44              : !  [[sqpopt_least_squares_module]]).
      45              : !
      46              : !  **Quasi-Newton Hessians.** The user's exact Hessian is sparse, and goes
      47              : !  into \( K \) as it is. A limited-memory quasi-Newton Hessian is not
      48              : !  sparse, but it is a multiple of the identity plus a matrix of low rank
      49              : !  `r` (see [[sqpopt_hessian_module]]),
      50              : !  \( B = \theta I + \sigma\, U M^{-1} U^T \). So
      51              : !  \( K = K_0 + \sigma\, E M^{-1} E^T \), where \( K_0 \) is the sparse
      52              : !  matrix with \( \theta I \) for the Hessian, and \( E \) is \( U \)
      53              : !  in the free variables (zero elsewhere). Only \( K_0 \) is factored, and:
      54              : !
      55              : !  * a solve uses the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury formula,
      56              : !    \( K^{-1} = K_0^{-1} - K_0^{-1} E\, T^{-1} E^T K_0^{-1} \), with the
      57              : !    small dense matrix \( T = \sigma M + E^T K_0^{-1} E \): two solves
      58              : !    with \( K_0 \) each, after `r` solves to form \( T \) when the
      59              : !    matrix is factored (as IPOPT does for its limited-memory option);
      60              : !  * the inertia follows from the two ways of eliminating a block of
      61              : !    \( \begin{bmatrix} K_0 & E \\ E^T & -\sigma M \end{bmatrix} \)
      62              : !    (the Haynsworth inertia additivity formula): the number of negative
      63              : !    eigenvalues of \( K \) is that of \( K_0 \), plus the number of
      64              : !    positive eigenvalues of \( T \), minus that of \( \sigma M \).
