       5              : !  Second-order correction (SOC) step, used by the line searches in
       6              : !  [[sqpopt_linesearch_module]] and by the trust-region step in
       7              : !  [[sqpopt_trust_region_module]] when a full step has been rejected.
       8              : !
       9              : !  For strongly nonlinear constraints, a genuinely good step `p` can be
      10              : !  rejected by the merit function or filter because the *linearized*
      11              : !  constraint prediction \( c + Jp \) differs from the true value
      12              : !  \( c(x+p) \) (the Maratos effect). The SOC step adds a small
      13              : !  correction `d` that accounts for the true constraint values at `x+p`,
      14              : !  reusing the Jacobian at `x` (no new derivative evaluations):
      15              : !
      16              : !  $$ J_S\, d = -\left( c_S(x+p) - b_S \right) $$
      17              : !
      18              : !  solved in the minimum-norm sense with `LSQR` (or directly, with
      19              : !  `options%direct_least_squares`: see [[sqpopt_least_squares_module]]).
      20              : !  Here `S` is the set of
      21              : !  constraints that are active in the linearization (\( c+Jp \) at a bound
      22              : !  `b`), or violated at `x+p` (with `b` the violated bound). The corrected
      23              : !  step `p+d` is then projected onto the variable bounds. The correction is
      24              : !  not used if it is larger than the step itself (it is meant to be a small
      25              : !  correction; a large one comes from a nearly singular \( J_S \)), or if
      26              : !  `LSQR` stops at its iteration limit.
      27              : !
      28              : !@note Variables at a bound at `x+p` are not held fixed in the solve for
      29              : !      `d`, so the final projection can undo part of the correction. (Holding
      30              : !      them fixed, or re-solving with the variables that the correction takes
      31              : !      outside their bounds held fixed, was tried: it saves function
      32              : !      evaluations on some problems, but makes the correction large where
      33              : !      the free variables' columns of \( J_S \) are small, e.g. TP13, and
      34              : !      sends TP116 to a different local solution.)
