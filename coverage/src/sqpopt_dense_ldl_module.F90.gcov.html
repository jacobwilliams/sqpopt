       5              : !  The dense backends of [[sqpopt_symmetric_solver_module]], for small
       6              : !  matrices: the matrix is stored dense (order `n`, so \( n^2 \) elements),
       7              : !  and factored by the Bunch-Kaufman \( LDL^T \) factorization (symmetric
       8              : !  pivoting, with 1 by 1 and 2 by 2 pivots; about \( n^3/3 \) operations).
       9              : !  That one factorization gives both the solves and the exact inertia: the
      10              : !  signs of the eigenvalues of `D`'s blocks (Sylvester's law). So, unlike
      11              : !  QDLDL, it always knows the inertia, also of a Hessian with zeros on its
      12              : !  diagonal, and, unlike MUMPS, it has almost no overhead per call. But
      13              : !  each factorization costs \( O(n^3) \): it is meant for small matrices,
      14              : !  and refuses to start above `dense_max_order` (which `solve` checks
      15              : !  first: a larger problem is invalid input with these solvers, see
      16              : !  `sqpopt_linear_solver_max_order`). There are two:
      17              : !
      18              : !  * [[sqpopt_dense_ldl_type]] (`options%linear_solver =
      19              : !    sqpopt_linear_solver_dense`): SQPOPT's own factorization,
      20              : !    [[dense_ldl_factor]] and [[dense_ldl_solve]]. Always available.
      21              : !  * [[sqpopt_lapack_ldl_type]] (`options%linear_solver =
      22              : !    sqpopt_linear_solver_lapack`): the same factorization by LAPACK's
      23              : !    `DSYTRF` and `DSYTRS`, whose blocked, BLAS-level code is faster on the
      24              : !    larger matrices. It needs a library built with LAPACK and BLAS (the
      25              : !    `HAS_LAPACK` preprocessor directive, and `-llapack -lblas`), in double
      26              : !    precision; without it, `sqpopt_has_lapack` is false and the option
      27              : !    can't be chosen. This module is the only one that refers to LAPACK.
      28              : !
      29              : !  Both store the factors in the same form (LAPACK's), so they differ only
      30              : !  in the two procedures that factor and solve; the inertia, and the
      31              : !  handling of a singular matrix, are shared ([[count_inertia]]). A
      32              : !  singular matrix is not an error: its zero eigenvalues (those of `D`'s
      33              : !  blocks below `dense_zero_tol` times the largest element) are counted
      34              : !  (`n_null`), and replaced in `D` by a large value, which makes that
      35              : !  component of a solution negligible, so that a solve returns one of the
      36              : !  solutions of a consistent system (as MUMPS's null-pivot detection does).
