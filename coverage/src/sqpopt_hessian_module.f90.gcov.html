       5              : !  Limited-memory quasi-Newton approximation to the Hessian of the
       6              : !  Lagrangian. Rather than storing a dense \( n \times n \) matrix, only
       7              : !  the last `max_history` step/gradient-change vector pairs \( (s,y) \)
       8              : !  are kept (`max_history` is a small constant, independent of `n`), and
       9              : !  Hessian(-inverse)-vector products are formed matrix-free using the
      10              : !  compact representations and the standard two-loop recursion. Supports
      11              : !  (Powell-damped) BFGS and SR1 updates.
      12              : !
      13              : !  The pairs are kept in a circular buffer (no data is moved when the
      14              : !  oldest pair is discarded), together with their inner products
      15              : !  \( S^TS \) and \( S^TY \), which are updated in `O(nk)` as each pair is
      16              : !  added. The small `2k x 2k` (BFGS) or `k x k` (SR1) middle matrix of the
      17              : !  compact representation is formed from them and LU-factored only when
      18              : !  the pairs or the scaling change, not on every product, so each
      19              : !  [[hessian_vector_product]] costs only `O(nk)`. The diagonal (see
      20              : !  [[hessian_diagonal]]) is likewise kept until the pairs or the scaling
      21              : !  change. The dense QP solver, which needs the whole matrix, gets it from
      22              : !  [[hessian_dense]].
      23              : !
      24              : !  **Exact mode** (see [[hessian_set_exact]]): instead of the quasi-Newton
      25              : !  approximation, the user's sparse Hessian of the Lagrangian is used,
      26              : !  with the values set at each major iteration by [[hessian_set_values]].
      27              : !  The products are formed from its nonzeros, plus a shift
      28              : !  \( \delta I \) (\( \delta \ge 0 \)), a simple *primal inertia
      29              : !  correction* for an indefinite Hessian (as in IPOPT and Uno, but without
      30              : !  factorizing): wherever the solver would restart a quasi-Newton
      31              : !  approximation ([[hessian_reset]]: the QP step was not a descent
      32              : !  direction, a step failed, or a run of steps was very short), \( \delta \)
      33              : !  is increased tenfold, from `shift_min` times the size of the Hessian's
      34              : !  largest element; at each new major iteration that follows a good step,
      35              : !  it is divided by 3 (and set to 0 once below that minimum). The quasi-Newton updates do nothing
      36              : !  in this mode. With `options%inertia_control`, the shift that an
      37              : !  iteration needs is instead found from a factorization (see
      38              : !  [[sqpopt_inertia_module]]), and only the increases after a failed step
      39              : !  or a run of very short ones are carried over to the next iteration.
      40              : !
      41              : !  The exact Hessian depends on the multipliers it is evaluated with. After
      42              : !  a step whose shift was large (`shift_dominant`), the QP's multipliers
      43              : !  mostly balance the shift, so [[sqpopt_iterate]] replaces them by a
      44              : !  least-squares estimate before evaluating the next Hessian (see
      45              : !  [[multiplier_estimate]]).
      46              : !
      47              : !  **For the sparse factorizations** (see [[sqpopt_kkt_module]]), the
      48              : !  quasi-Newton matrix is available in its compact form, a multiple of the
      49              : !  identity plus a matrix of low rank `r`:
      50              : !
      51              : !  $$ B = \theta I + \sigma\, U M^{-1} U^T $$
      52              : !
      53              : !  with \( U = [\theta S \; Y] \), \( \sigma = -1 \) (BFGS, `r = 2k`) or
      54              : !  \( U = Y - \theta S \), \( \sigma = +1 \) (SR1, `r = k`), and the middle
      55              : !  matrix \( M \) of [[factor_middle_matrix]]: see [[hessian_low_rank_size]],
      56              : !  [[hessian_low_rank_multiply]], [[hessian_low_rank_column]], and
      57              : !  [[hessian_low_rank_middle]]. The shift
      58              : !  \( \delta I \) also applies to the quasi-Newton matrices (it is zero
      59              : !  unless the inertia control sets it, for SR1).
