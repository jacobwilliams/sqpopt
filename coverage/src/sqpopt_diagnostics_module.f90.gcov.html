       5              : !  Diagnostics of a solve, to help when it doesn't converge (see
       6              : !  `options%diagnostic_level`). The iteration log says what happened at
       7              : !  each iteration; the diagnostics say which constraints and variables are
       8              : !  responsible, and what the final status most likely means. They are
       9              : !  opt-in, by level:
      10              : !
      11              : !  * `1`: a diagnosis of the final point ([[diagnostics_finish]]): the
      12              : !    constraints with the largest violations and multipliers, the variables
      13              : !    with the largest stationarity residuals, the active set and its
      14              : !    degeneracy (multipliers that are zero or have the wrong sign, active
      15              : !    gradients that are linearly dependent), the last QP subproblem (its
      16              : !    elastic constraints), and a verdict for the final status. It uses the
      17              : !    values the solver has at the final point anyway.
      18              : !  * `2`: also a report on the starting point ([[diagnostics_start]]: the
      19              : !    sizes of the gradients, which show how the problem is scaled, and the
      20              : !    structure of the constraints), and what the iterations showed: the
      21              : !    rate of convergence, the step lengths, a working set that flips between
      22              : !    two states, the iteration that took the most time
      23              : !    ([[diagnostics_record]]), and constraints whose changes along the steps
      24              : !    disagree with their derivatives ([[diagnostics_point]]). With
      25              : !    `options%diagnostics_unit`, the history of the iterations is also
      26              : !    written to a file, as comma-separated values.
      27              : !  * `3`: also the diagnostics that evaluate the user's functions: `f` and
      28              : !    `c` at two points next to the starting point, to see whether it is at
      29              : !    the edge of their domain.
      30              : !
      31              : !  Levels `0` to `2` call the user's functions exactly as often as a solve
      32              : !  without diagnostics: everything is computed from values the solver has
      33              : !  evaluated (through the problem's evaluation caches), and no level
      34              : !  changes the iterates. The results are returned in
      35              : !  `results%diagnosis` (see [[sqpopt_diagnosis_type]]), whose `report` and
      36              : !  `problem_report` are the texts the solver prints (if
      37              : !  `options%print_level >= 1`).
      38              : !
      39              : !  **The derivative check of level 2** needs no evaluations. Between two
      40              : !  consecutive iterates \( x \) and \( x^+ = x + s \), the change of a
      41              : !  function is \( \tfrac12 (\nabla c(x) + \nabla c(x^+))^T s \) up to a term
      42              : !  of the third order in \( s \) (the trapezoid rule, exact for a quadratic),
      43              : !  and the solver has the values and the derivatives at both. A constraint
      44              : !  (or the objective) whose actual changes differ from this by more than
      45              : !  20% in most of the steps has derivatives that don't match its values.
      46              : !  Only steps that change no variable by more than 10% of its size are
      47              : !  used, so that the third-order term is small.
